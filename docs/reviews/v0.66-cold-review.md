## FALSE: the parity-table headline contradicts the release's own scorecard

`CHANGELOG.md`, fifteen lines apart, in the same `[0.66.0]` section:

> line 148–149: "`known_open_pins` went **104 -> 27 -> 127**. It **CLOSED 77**
> wrong-answer pins and ADDED 100 newly-measured ones…"
>
> line 162: "**84 of 89** pinned parity entries — **585 of 595** assertions —
> went from a WRONG ANSWER to correct-or-refused."

These describe the *same event*. The selector-parity table
(`scripts/repro/selector_parity_197_differential.py`'s `KNOWN` dict) is where
essentially the entire 104→27 fall came from: extracting the dict via
`ast.literal_eval` at the `v0.65.0` tag gives **103 entries** total (including
14 unrelated `#539-grow-fails` entries, excluded by the release's own
convention), and the other three original `known_open_pins` tables
(`arm_corpus_sweep`'s `KNOWN_ARM_MISMATCHES`, and the two home-alias tables)
together held exactly **1** entry — 104 − 103 = 1. At `HEAD`, the same
extraction gives **26** entries in the parity table, and the same arithmetic
(27 − 26 = 1) says the other three tables are unchanged. So the parity
table's own entry count moved **103 → 26, a fall of 77** — matching "CLOSED
77" exactly, and contradicting "84 of 89" in the very same document.

Breaking the parity table down by issue (both extractions independent,
`v0.65.0` tag vs. `HEAD`):

| issue | v0.65.0 tag | HEAD |
|---|---|---|
| `#1204` | 6 entries / 15 assertions | **6 / 15 — unchanged** |
| `#1206` | 1 entry / 2 assertions | **1 / 2 — unchanged** |
| `#1209` | 42 / 513 | 0 |
| `#1210` | 22 / 33 | 0 |
| `#1211` | 6 / 10 | 0 |
| `#1214` | 1 / 1 | 0 |
| `#1215` | 11 / 21 | 5 / 11 |
| `#539-grow-fails` (excluded) | 14 / 27 | 14 / 27 |
| **issue-tracked total** | **89 / 595** | **12 / 28** |

`#1209`, `#1210`, `#1211` and `#1214` genuinely went to zero, and `#1215` went
11→5 — that part of the work is real: **77 entries (567 assertions)** of
correct-or-refused closure. But the true residual is **12 entries / 28
assertions across three issues**, not the claimed "5 entries / 11
assertions." `#1204` and `#1206` are untouched by this release — confirmed
live: running `selector_parity_197_differential.py` against a freshly built
`HEAD` binary still prints `control_nested_select.wast … [optimized: R11
clobbered: 0x00000001 != 0x20000100]` (`#1204`) and `control_loop.wast m0
countdown(0x5): … optimized=timeout` (`#1206`) — and neither is disclosed in
the "Known residuals this release does NOT claim to have closed" list, nor
anywhere else in the `[0.66.0]` section. (`#1204` is named once, in passing,
in the unrelated mutation-survey discussion in the leftover `[Unreleased]`
section — acknowledged as a live pin there, never connected to the parity
headline. `#1206` is not mentioned anywhere outside the historical `v0.65.0`
section.)

The likely mechanism: `RQ-66-BOTHWRONG`'s own scope is five *issues*
(`#1209`/`#1210`/`#1211`/`#1214`/`#1215`), which pre-fix total **82 entries /
578 assertions** (42+22+6+1+11, 513+33+10+1+21) — not 89/595. The artifact's
`verified-by:` text (`artifacts/release-v0.66/RQ-66-BOTHWRONG.yaml:108–111`)
says "the five issues' pins fell from 89 entries / 595 assertions to 5
entries / 11 assertions" — mislabeling the *whole tracked population*
(which also holds `#1204` and `#1206`, untouched by this PR) as if it were
solely "the five issues'" total. There is also a bare arithmetic slip
independent of that: even taking "5 entries / 11 assertions" at face value,
89 − 5 = 84 works, but 595 − 11 = **584**, not 585.

**This appears in three places, all needing the same correction**:
`CHANGELOG.md:162`, `artifacts/release-v0.66/RQ-66-BOTHWRONG.yaml` (both
`landed:` at line 65-66 and `verified-by:` at line 108-111), and PR #1251's
body. The corrected sentence: *"77 of 89 pinned parity entries (567 of 595
assertions) moved from a wrong answer to correct-or-refused; residual is 12
entries / 28 assertions across `#1204` (6/15), `#1206` (1/2) and `#1215`
(5/11) — not 5/11."* This is a prose/accounting defect, not a code defect:
no compiler byte is wrong as a result of it, and `#1204`/`#1206` were
already-known, pre-existing defects before this release. But it is the
release's lead technical correctness claim, it is wrong, and the release's
own document already states the right number fifteen lines above it.
